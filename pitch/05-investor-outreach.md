# Brücke — Investor Outreach Kit

*Compiled 2026-10-08 · v0.1*

*How to run the pre-seed process, who to contact, and what to send. Verify every name/firm on OpenVC, Crunchbase, and Signal before sending; rosters change fast. These are start points for research, not a finished map.*

---

## Process mechanics (2026 norms)

- **Warm intro > cold email.** Build an intro chain first: advisors, accelerator alumni, language-learning founder angels. Ask each advisor/target for 2–3 intros, not "any intros."
- **Raise in parallel, close fast.** Target a 4–6 week process. Tell investors "we're meeting 25 investors over 4 weeks; first close is [date]."
- **Materials:** 12-slide deck and 1-page exec summary (you have both), 3-min investor demo cut, metrics one-pager, SAFE with a cap. No data room needed pre-seed.
- **Weekly pipeline tracker:** investor → warmth → status → blockers. Momentum information ("fund X asked for data yesterday") is your best closing tool.

### Angels with unfair-advantage audiences

- **Language-learning founder/operators** — ex-Duolingo, Preply, Speak, Babbel, Busuu, LingQ people; they know distribution and pricing.
- **Polyglot-creator money** — polyglots who invest (search "polyglot angel investor"; several mid-size creators run syndicates) — an investment from a creator is also a marketing campaign.
- **Typing/keyboard community angels** — the Monkeytype adjacency is real; local-first, beautiful-tools brands resonate with this crowd.
- **German/DACH diaspora founders** in your region — ties to the content market and possibly grants.

### Syndicates & platforms

- AngelList syndicates focused on edtech/consumer subscription
- OpenVC, Signal, Crunchbase to build the verified list; Republic/Wefunder if you do the crowdfund route (Fluent Forever precedent on Republic).

## The 10-minute talk track (10 beats)

1. "You already understand more German than you think" — do water→Wasser live.
2. The nine laws → deduction, not memorization.
3. Why now: AI-content trust collapse + Duolingo's quality gap.
4. Demo the trail map & one production drill.
5. The verification moat (every etymology sourced; enforced by the test suite).
6. Market: $1.5B apps / $21B+ TAM; German = top employment-migration language.
7. Business model and the ~zero-infra-cost margin story.
8. Moat: the sourced compendium; multi-language leverage on the shared engine.
9. Traction numbers + the roadmap to seed metrics.
10. Ask: $350K, allocation, milestones, 18-month horizon.

## Diligence prep checklist (build before first meeting)

- [ ] Metrics dashboard (retention curves, activation, lesson funnels) — read-only demo link
- [ ] Cap table (clean, founder-majority), SAFE templates, incorporation docs
- [ ] Demo video: investor demo cut (3 min) + 5 screenshots
- [ ] Product tour script for a live session (10 minutes, prepared path: map → lesson → drill → review)
- [ ] IP & content provenance memo: sources and licenses for themes (Monkeytype ports), fonts, sounds; Language Transfer credit posture
- [ ] 3 user testimonials / community threads
- [ ] 18-month financial model (simple: content, growth, product; breakeven path)
- [ ] Risk memos: Duolingo, solo-founder, efficacy

## If the round stalls (decision tree)

- Pitch retention is weak → fix onboarding before more outreach (retention is the product's whole story).
- Investors say "crowdfunding first" → do the Kickstarter; a funded campaign converts skeptics into believers and pre-sells the seed.
- Accelerators pass → angels + micro-VC at $250–350K instead; re-apply to YC next batch with more metrics.
- Truly no pull → cut burn to personal-funds level, run the community launch, return in 2 quarters with D30 data. The product is built; time is on your side.
