You're right, and I'm going to be direct with you rather than give you a generic "10 marketing tips" list.

## The honest diagnosis

You don't have a marketing problem. You have a **distribution problem** with an **awareness problem**. Those are different things, and the order matters.

**Your actual asset isn't the app — it's the insight.** "English and German are the same language that forked 1200 years ago, and here are the 9 rules that connect them" is a genuinely fascinating idea that most people don't know. The app is just the best container for that insight. Lead with the insight, not the app.

## What I would NOT do

**Don't raise capital.** Not now. Investors fund traction, not products. If you walk into a room and say "I built a beautiful language app with zero users," you'll get a polite no and give away equity for nothing. Capital comes *after* you have numbers that prove people care.

**Don't pay for marketing.** Paid ads for a free/open-source learning app with no revenue model is burning money. You need organic signal first.

**Don't cold-email the Goethe-Institut yet.** Institutions move at glacial speed and want proof before they'll touch you. Come back to them with 500 active users and they'll actually listen.

## What I think actually works — in order

### 1. Content marketing around the INSIGHT (week 1-2)

The sound shift thesis is **inherently viral content**. Not the app — the *idea*.

- A short post: "The word 'water' → 'Wasser'. 'Pepper' → 'Pfeffer'. 'Pound' → 'Pfund'. English P became German PF around 600 AD, and once you see the pattern, you can't unsee it." — That's a post people share.
- **Where**: Reddit ([r/languagelearning](https://reddit.com/r/languagelearning) has 1.3M members, [r/German](https://reddit.com/r/German) has 400K+), Hacker News (they love well-engineered solo projects), Twitter/X, and YouTube Shorts/TikTok (a 60-second "did you know" video).
- **Don't pitch the app.** Teach the shift. Put a link in your bio/profile. People who find it fascinating will click.

### 2. Write a Show HN post (week 1)

Hacker News is *exactly* your audience: technically literate people who appreciate well-engineered things and love learning. A Show HN with the title **"Brücke — Learn German through the 1,200-year-old sound shifts that connect it to English"** with a honest, technical description of the approach + a link to the live site has a real shot at the front page. HN has sent many solo projects from 0 to thousands of users overnight.

Your AGENTS.md, your testing philosophy, your zero-dependency stance — HN eats this up.

### 3. The polyglot community (week 2-4)

Your instinct here is correct. **This is the right community.** But don't ask them for advice — **give them something to react to.**

- Send a DM to 5-10 polyglot YouTubers/creators with a 2-sentence pitch + link. Not "please promote my app" — instead: "I built a free tool that teaches German through the consonant shift. I'd genuinely love your take on whether the pedagogy works." Language nerds can't resist this.
- Post in polyglot Discord servers and forums. The Refold community, Language Learners Forum, etc.
- The r/languagelearning subreddit has "Resource" flair for exactly this.

### 4. Tell your friends (week 0 — literally today)

> "I have not even told my friends yet about this"

This is the real blocker. If you can't pitch it to people who already like you, you're not going to pitch it to strangers. Send 10 people the link tonight. Watch them use it. Their confusion points are your marketing copy. Their "oh cool" moments are your hooks.

### 5. The co-founder question

Finding a marketing co-founder is the **highest-leverage move** but also the **highest-risk**. A bad co-founder is worse than no co-founder. Here's my take:

- **Yes** if you find someone who is genuinely obsessed with language education or EdTech and has *demonstrated* distribution ability (their own audience, previous launches, content track record)
- **No** if it's just "my friend is good at talking to people" — that's not marketing, that's vibes
- **Alternative**: find a content creator in the language space who'd be excited to collaborate on content (not equity, just mutual interest). If that works, *then* talk about formalizing it.

## The uncomfortable truth

The first 100 users are the hardest and there's no trick. It's you, manually, putting the insight in front of people who care about languages, one community at a time. The good news: your product is genuinely differentiated. You're not selling "another flashcard app." You're selling "a key that unlocks hundreds of German words you already know." That's a story people *want* to tell each other — you just have to tell it first.

**Start tonight. Tell 10 friends. Post on r/languagelearning tomorrow. Write your Show HN draft this weekend.** The app is ready. You're the bottleneck now.
