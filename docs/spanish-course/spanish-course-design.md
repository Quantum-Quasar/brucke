# El Puente — the Spanish course design

> The Spanish sibling of Brücke (German: *die Brücke*) and Le Pont (French: *le pont*). Same app,
> same method, same bridge — crossed from the Latinate Spanish side. This document is the Thinking
> Method blueprint for the Spanish course: what it teaches, in what order, and why. Every lesson in
> `src/data/spanish/lessons-es.ts` must be justifiable against this document and against the
> guidebook's ten principles (`thinking-method.md` §1.1–§1.10).

## 0. What the method demands (non-negotiables)

1. **We import knowledge.** The learner already owns thousands of Spanish words through English's
   deep Latinate vocabulary. The course never "teaches vocabulary" it can *convert*. The bridge:
   English absorbed a vast Latin vocabulary directly and through Norman French. Spanish kept those
   same Latin stems alive in everyday speech.
2. **We reframe.** Spanish's core machinery is reframed as sense, not memory:
   - **Gender is not memorized** — it is *decoded from word endings*. The `-ción/-dad` squad is
     invariably feminine (`la`); words in `-o` are masculine (`el`), words in `-a` feminine (`la`).
   - **Pronouns are articles on the move.** `lo/la` are introduced as "it" right from topic 1:
     `lo quiero` ("I want it"), `la quiero` ("I want it [the confirmation]"). The pronoun walks
     one step left before conjugated verbs, but hooks to the tail of infinitives: `quiero invitarlo`.
   - **Negation is the single word 'no'.** Unlike English's "do not" and French's two-part sandwich
     `ne...pas`, Spanish simply places `no` before the verb: `no quiero`, `no lo quiero`.
   - **Ser vs. Estar is characteristic vs. state.** Not "permanent vs. temporary" (a misleading
     shortcut warned against in `thinking-method.md` §1.2), but *what something is* (essence/identity)
     versus *how or where something is* (state/location).
   - **The `-ado/-ido` past is the English `-ed` past.** `confirmado`, `celebrado`, `decidido`.
3. **We weave.** Threads planted early pay off later:
   - `quiero` (I want) and `lo` (it) in topic 1 establish pronoun placement.
   - The `-ción` engine in topic 2 unlocks hundreds of feminine nouns.
   - The `-ar` engine in topic 3 turns those nouns into infinitives, which topics 12 (near future),
     17 (perfect), and 25 (gustar) harvest.
4. **One thought at a time.** No exercise combines two unfamiliar structures at once.
5. **We mask repetition.** Drills rotate surfaces, subjects, and polarity without prose repetition.
6. **We cue, correct, and raise consciousness.** Trap notes preempt false friends (*actualmente*
   is "currently", *embarazada* is "pregnant", *librería* is "bookshop").

## 1. The 9 Shift Families (The Atlas)

1. **`cion` — The -CIÓN Passport:** `-tion` → `-ción` (all feminine `la`: `la invitación`, `la confirmación`).
2. **`dad` — The -DAD Quality Bridge:** `-ty` → `-dad` (all feminine `la`: `la ciudad`, `la realidad`).
3. **`mente` — The -MENTE Adverb Engine:** `-ly` → feminine adjective + `-mente` (`rápidamente`, `naturalmente`).
4. **`ar_engine` — The -ar Verb Factory:** `-ation` nouns / `-ate` verbs → `-ar` (`invitar`, `celebrar`, `organizar`).
5. **`ico` — The -ICO Bridge:** `-ic/-ical` → `-ico/-ica` (always carrying an acute accent: `mágico`, `público`).
6. **`al` — The -AL Invariable Passport:** `-al` unchanged (`natural`, `central`, `formal`, `personal`).
7. **`f_to_h` — Latin Initial F- → Spanish Silent H-:** Latin `f-` → Spanish `h-` (`hacer` ↔ fact, `hijo` ↔ filial).
8. **`ct_to_ch` — Latin -CT- → Spanish -CH-:** Latin `-ct-` → Spanish `-ch-` (`noche` ↔ nocturnal, `leche` ↔ lactose).
9. **`pl_cl_fl_to_ll` — Latin Initial PL-/CL-/FL- → Spanish LL-:** Latin `pl-/cl-/fl-` → Spanish `ll-` (`llave` ↔ clef, `llamar` ↔ claim).

## 2. Course Architecture (30 Topics)

### Phase 1 — The Decode Engine
1. **The Spanish Inside Your English** — `el/la`, `quiero`, `quieres`, `lo` as it, question by intonation.
2. **The -CIÓN Bridge** — `-ción` nouns convert (all feminine `la`); `la` = feminine it.
3. **The -ar Engine: Verbs from Nouns** — `-ción` → `-ar`; `quiero + infinitivo`; the `celebrar` trap.
4. **Negation: The Clean 'No'** — `no quiero`, `no lo quiero`; placing `no` directly before the verb cluster.
5. **Ser & Estar: The Two Beings** — `es` (characteristic) vs `está` (state/location); `muy`.
6. **The -dad & -mente Bridges** — `-ty` → `-dad` (feminine!); `-ly` → `-mente` adverbs.
7. **Questions: Inverted & Intonation** — `¿?` punctuation, `dónde`, `qué`, `por qué` vs `porque`.

### Phase 2 — The Sentence Machine
8. **The -ico & -al Bridges** — `-ico` (esdrújula accent) and invariable `-al`.
9. **tú & usted** — The T-V address line; `por favor`, `gracias`, greetings.
10. **tener & hay** — Possession (`tengo`) vs existence (`hay` from haber).
11. **Adjective Agreement** — Masculine `-o`, feminine `-a`, plural `-s`.
12. **The Near Future** — `ir a + infinitivo`: `voy a visitar`.
13. **por vs para** — Cause/exchange (`por`) vs purpose/destination (`para`).
14. **Possessives Agree with the Thing** — `mi`, `tu`, `su`, `mis`, `tus`, `sus`.

### Phase 3 — Time Travel
15. **Numbers & Ages** — 1–100; `tengo 30 años` (age as possession).
16. **Days, Months & Dates** — `lunes`, `en enero`, `hoy`, `mañana`.
17. **The -ado / -ido Past** — Pretérito perfecto con haber: `he confirmado`, `he comido`.
18. **The Preterite: The Dot in Time** — Defined past events: `hablé`, `comí`, `fue`.
19. **The Imperfect: The Storytelling Past** — Continuous background: `-aba` and `-ía`.
20. **desde hace & Present Duration** — Present tense for ongoing actions with duration.
21. **Comparatives: más, menos, mejor** — `más que`, `menos que`, irregular `mejor/peor`.

### Phase 4 — The Deep Engine
22. **Double Object Pronouns** — Pronouns before verb (`me lo da`), `le lo` → `se lo`.
23. **Direct vs Indirect Objects** — `lo/la` (direct) vs `le/les` (to him/her/them).
24. **Reflexives & Daily Life** — `me llamo`, `levantarse`, daily round.
25. **Gustar: The Inverted Liking** — `me gusta` ("it pleases me"), `te gusta`.
26. **The Would-World & Subjunctive Preview** — `me gustaría`, `quiero que vengas`.

### Phase 5 — Synthesis
27. **The Silent H & Palatal Shifts** — Historical shifts: `f-` → `h-`, `-ct-` → `-ch-`, `pl-` → `ll-`.
28. **que & quien** — Relative pronouns and connectors.
29. **Spanish Idioms as Thought** — `dar en el clavo`, `tomar el pelo`, `meter la pata`.
30. **Capstone: El Puente Reading** — Connected reading passage synthesizing all conversion bridges.
